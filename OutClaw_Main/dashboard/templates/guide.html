OutClaw — A Simple Guide

Everything on one page. No complicated words. If you get stuck, start here.

1What OutClaw does

OutClaw checks your court papers for problems before you file them.

Judges expect the cases and laws you mention in your papers to be real, and to say what you say they say. Sometimes a paper has a mistake in it — a case that does not exist, a case that has been overruled, or a law that is being used to mean the opposite of what it really means.

OutClaw reads your papers and looks for those kinds of problems. It cannot tell you whether you will win. It is a checking tool — like spell-check, but for the facts in your court papers.

2How to use it

There is one page and one big button. Here is the whole process:

  1. Add your papers. Click the box in the middle of the page, then pick the files from your computer. (You can also drag them onto the box.) PDF files and plain text files work best.
  2. Press the big green button that says “Check My Papers.”
  3. Wait about a minute. Do not close the window. A circle will spin while it works.
  4. Read the answer. You will get one of three results — red, yellow, or green — with a plain-English explanation under it.

That is it. You can check as many papers as you want, one at a time or several at once.

3What the colors mean

Think of it like a traffic light for your papers.

🔴

Red — stop

OutClaw found a serious problem. If you file these papers the way they are, it could hurt your case.

🟡

Yellow — look closer

OutClaw found possible problems. They might be fixable, but a judge could see them as mistakes.

🟢

Green — no problems found

OutClaw did not find citation problems in these papers. This is not a guarantee — it means nothing stood out to the check.

4What to do with each result

🔴

If you got red

Do not file these papers yet.

Read the list of problems under the big result. Each one is written in plain words, with the exact sentence from your papers and the case or law it is about.

What to do: Before you file, fix each problem or have a real attorney (or a free legal-aid clinic) look at it. There is help available that costs nothing — many courthouses and law libraries can point you to it.

🟡

If you got yellow

Review the items before filing.

The problems may be small and fixable — like a citation that does not quite support the sentence. The page tells you which sentences to look at.

What to do: Look at each flagged sentence. Fix what you can. If you are not sure whether something is a real problem, show it to someone qualified before you file.

🟢

If you got green

Nothing stood out.

What to do: Green does not mean “perfect.” It means OutClaw did not find citation problems. If this is an important filing, it is still a good idea to have a qualified person review it before you submit it.

5If it says your papers could not be read

Sometimes OutClaw cannot open a file — for example if a PDF is damaged or is just a scanned picture with no text in it. When that happens, OutClaw will not pretend everything is fine. It will tell you clearly that it could not check that file.

What to do: Open that file yourself, save it again (as a PDF or plain text), and try again. Do not file anything that has not been checked.

Your papers stay on your computer. OutClaw runs on the machine you are using right now. Nothing you check is uploaded or shared — unless someone turns on the optional AI helper (which is off by default, and only ever uses free services you choose).
Important: OutClaw is a checking tool, not a lawyer. It looks for common citation problems. It cannot give legal advice, and it cannot tell you whether you will win or lose. Before you file anything that matters, have a real attorney or a free legal-aid clinic look at it too. You do not have to face this alone — free help exists in every state.
